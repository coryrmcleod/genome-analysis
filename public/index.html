    03 · INTERPRETATION

    Evidence before conclusions.

    Three review tiers separate established clinical relevance, context-dependent associations, and exploratory findings. They are editorial priorities, not a universal gene score. A gene may contain many variants with different effects.

    Trait groups organize related study endpoints. They do not sum variant effects: nearby variants may reflect the same signal, and study scales differ. Polygenic scores and percentiles require a validated model, suitable genotype coverage, and a compatible reference population. Published raw score models are installed separately. Compatible models can show empirical European reference-sample percentiles; incomplete scores use explicitly hypothetical ranks and possible ranges. These ranks are not calibrated disease risks. Incomplete inputs show a matched-variant subtotal and a separately labeled population-genotype what-if estimate or range. APOE types, CYP star alleles, penetrance, and absolute disease risk are not inferred. A reference call does not rule out disease. Confirm consequential findings with a qualified clinical service.

    04 · POPULATION CONTEXT

    Common where, and in whom?

    The starter panel uses 1000 Genomes Phase 3 EUR allele counts through Ensembl. It is a reference sample, not every European population and not a measurement of your ancestry.

    Frequency refers to the explicitly named tracked allele, not the frequency of your genotype. Missing frequency means unknown. No frequency is substituted from another population.
